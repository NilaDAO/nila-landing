# W4 Impact — what peers working with smallholder women farmers state, and how

Research note for Carst, 10 Nov 2026. Sources read on 10 Nov 2026 (list at the end). Purpose: find the data points (subcategories) for the W4 rings. Each statement is quoted or summarised from the source; "method" is what the source itself says about how it was measured.

## What they state

| Organisation | Statement (as published) | Metric | Method stated |
|---|---|---|---|
| **One Acre Fund** (Africa, 2023 report) | "$143 per farmer" new profits and assets (full-service); "$3.20:1" return per $1; 95% loan repayment | US$ per farmer; ratio; % repaid | Harvests **physically weighed** for clients and neighbouring control farmers; external evaluations referenced |
| One Acre Fund | Veterans "52% more likely" to have maize left in the hunger season; 25% more organic inputs; 13–25% more assets | % difference vs newly enrolled | Veteran vs new client comparison, method not detailed |
| **Root Capital** (via ImpactAlpha, 2022) | Women-led agribusinesses earned "$17,850 more in profits per loan"; lower write-downs | US$ per loan | Lender's own portfolio data, correlation |
| **60 Decibels** farmer benchmarks (2023, 93 projects, 18,000+ farmers) | % of farmers reporting improved practices, production, earnings, quality of life. Women vs men, e.g. earnings 27% vs 25% | % self-reporting "significantly" better | Interviews with farmers, self-reported, "in their own words" |
| **CARE She Feeds the World** (2023 brief) | Peru: household income US$328 → US$441 a month (+34.5%); ~80% of women gained decision power over investments; food insecurity 53% → 37.6% (FAO FIES). Uganda: women owning assets 19% → ~83%; women controlling sale of their produce 43.6% | US$ income, % of women, FIES score | Baseline vs endline; Peru final evaluation; sample sizes not given |
| **Heifer International** | "A living income means thriving, not just surviving" | Income against a living-income benchmark per person per day (e.g. $2.15 to $5.34, Dec 2024) | Anker method: desk review, focus groups, market prices; revised at least every 5 years |
| **IFPRI pro-WEAI** (the standard women's empowerment index) | A woman is empowered in at least 8 of 10 indicators | 10 indicators: autonomy in income, self-efficacy, attitudes to intimate partner violence, input in livelihood decisions, ownership of land and assets, access to and decisions on credit, control over use of income, work balance, visiting important places, group membership | Household survey, an adequacy cutoff per indicator |
| **IRIS+** (GIIN impact metrics) | Average client agriculture yield: smallholder (PI9421, to be replaced by PI5935) | Yield per hectare | Reporting standard, method left to the reporter |
| **PRADAN** (India, 7 states) | "1 million women organized into 86,711 SHGs"; "2,537,200 families food-secure year round"; water models raise incomes "by 75–100%"; 9% of households above ₹2.5 lakh a year | Counts of women, families, households; income bands | Not stated; scopes and dates differ across figures |
| **DHAN Foundation** (Madurai, Tamil Nadu; 2025 report) | 6,50,271 member families "moved out of poverty" (cumulative); 22,557 in 2024–25; 36,401 women farmers in FPOs; cultivation cost cut "up to 13%" | Families self-declared out of poverty; members; % cost | **Self-declared by the family, confirmed by the group** (community validation); others from programme records |

## What this means for the W4 subcategories

1. **Peers mostly publish counts and self-reported change, without the method beside the number.** Only One Acre Fund weighs harvests against control farmers. A figure on nila.land with its source and method beside it (block, satellite record, survey wording) is already stronger than most of the field.
2. **Income has three accepted forms:** a gain against a comparison group (One Acre Fund), before and after (CARE), or the share above a living income (Heifer). For Tamil Nadu, DHAN's "self-declared moved out of poverty, confirmed by the group" fits unions that already meet as groups.
3. **Women's agency has a standard: pro-WEAI.** Three of its ten indicators fit what Nila records or can ask simply: ownership of land (her title), access to and decisions on credit (her crop share, her signature), group membership (every member is in an SHG). Control over use of income needs a question.
4. **Food is stated as food security, not yield**, by CARE (FAO Food Insecurity Experience Scale, a short survey) and One Acre Fund (food left in the hunger season). Seasons cropped and satellite yield are Nila's own; nobody in this set publishes them per field.
5. **"Quality of life better" is the 60 Decibels question.** Their benchmark gives a comparison: about a third of farmers report improvement across agriculture projects.
6. **Repayment is stated by lenders (One Acre Fund 95%).** Nila's 39 of 39 closed sits in that row, with the same caveat on the running book.

## Doubts

1. Which income form: comparison group, before and after, living income, or self-declared out of poverty? They need different data; one is enough to start.
2. A survey sits behind four candidate rings (income, her voice, food security, control over income). One short survey in the app or at the union meeting, once a season?
3. Living income for rural Tamil Nadu: Heifer's benchmark set does not include India; the Anker-based figures found so far cover Nilgiris and Tiruppur only.
